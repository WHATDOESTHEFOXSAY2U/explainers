01

Something shakes. It shoves the air.

The air only wobbles in place. The squeeze travels. That is sound.

watch the last squeeze leave

How do you draw that?

02

Stand in one spot. Plot the squeeze over time.

Squeezed: up. Stretched: down. That line is a waveform.

A simple wave has only two knobs.

03

How fast. How big.

Wiggles per second = frequency = pitch. Height = amplitude = loudness.

What if two waves share the same air?

04

Waves just add.

Lined up, they double. Opposite, they cancel into silence.

Real instruments stack many waves at once.

05

Harmonics: one note is a stack.

A string wiggles as a whole, in halves, in thirds… all at once: 1×, 2×, 3× the base frequency.

Mix them. Same pitch, different recipe: flute vs violin.

Each of those is a pure tone. Can you go backwards, from the wave to the recipe?

06

Fourier transform: un-mix the wave.

Hand it any wave. It tells you which pure tones are inside, and how much of each.

6.1What it does

Like a prism splitting white light into its colours.

6.2Ask one question

“Any 440 Hz in here?” Multiply the wave by a 440 Hz test tone, then add it all up.

Why that works. A matching frequency rises and falls with the test tone: mostly green. Any other frequency drifts in and out of step: equal green and red, which cancel to 0.

6.3Keep score

What is left after red cancels green is the amplitude of that frequency: 1.00 = a full-size tone, 0 = not there. That is the bar's height.

mix

You mixed in 1.00 of 220 Hz and 0.50 of 660 Hz. Those are exactly the bars you get back.

6.4By hand

This wave is 1 Hz at amplitude 2 plus 3 Hz at amplitude 5. Can 12 samples find that?

6.5The spectrum

More questions, finer chart. That bar chart is the spectrum.

6.6What it's for

Hard on the wave, easy on the bars: remove a whine.

  • Tunerstallest bar = the note
  • Equalisersturn bars up or down
  • Noise removaldelete unwanted bars
  • MP3drop bars you can't hear
  • Shazam, voice AIrecognise the bar pattern

One sound, two pictures.

07

Waveform shows when. Spectrum shows what.

But a spectrum forgets the order. Reverse the tune and it doesn't notice.

The fix: don't transform the whole thing at once.

08

Spectrogram: slice, transform, stack.

Slide along, slice by slice. Now you see what and when.

drag to move the slice

x = time · y = frequency · colour = amplitude

One last tweak, for human ears.

09

Mel: redraw the axis the way ears hear.

Ears don't hear in Hz. The mel scale is a ruler where equal steps sound equal. A mel spectrogram is a spectrogram with its y axis on that ruler.

9.1Hear the problem

Both jump by the same 300 Hz. One leaps, one barely moves.

9.2Two staircases

Only the mel one sounds even.

9.3The mel ruler

Hz in, mel out. Steep at the low end, so low frequencies get most of the ruler.

Dashed lines: equal mel steps.

9.4Bands on one slice

Cut the slice's bars into bands: thin down low, wide up high. Each band's bars merge into one.

9.5Every slice

Each column shrinks from 513 bars to 40 bands, and the low end gets the room.

Why bother. It is what speech and music AI models are fed instead of raw sound.

Now try it on your own voice.
